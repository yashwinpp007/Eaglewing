How to Choose a Commercial Cleaning Company in Melbourne

By the EagleWing Facility Services Team

FEATURED IMAGE — COMMERCIAL CLEANING MELBOURNE

Choosing the wrong commercial cleaning company costs more than the contract. Missed tasks, liability gaps, staff turnover, and the administrative overhead of constant follow-up — the real cost is rarely visible in the quote. Here is the framework we recommend every Melbourne facility manager use before signing.

1. Verify Insurance Before Anything Else

The most expensive mistake a facility manager can make is hiring an underinsured cleaning contractor. At minimum, require:

  • Public liability insurance — $20 million minimum. Some facilities (hospitals, government buildings) require $50M+.
  • Workers compensation — Legally mandatory for employees in Victoria. If a cleaner is injured on your site and the company has no workers comp, your business may bear liability.
  • Professional indemnity insurance — Relevant if the cleaner provides facility management or consultancy services in addition to cleaning.

Always ask for certificates of currency — documents issued by the insurer confirming current coverage. A verbal confirmation is not sufficient. Check expiry dates.

2. Confirm Direct Employment vs. Subcontracting

This is the single most important question to ask any commercial cleaning company: "Are the staff who will clean my facility direct employees of your company?"

Many cleaning companies operate as brokers — they win contracts and subcontract the work to independent operators. This creates accountability gaps:

  • Subcontractors may not be trained to the company's stated standards
  • Insurance coverage may not extend to subcontractors on your site
  • Staff turnover is typically higher, meaning unfamiliar faces and inconsistent results
  • Escalations go through an extra layer, slowing response times

A company that employs its staff directly carries full employer obligations — superannuation, workers compensation, training accountability — and has far greater control over service quality.

3. Evaluate Technology and Proof of Service

In 2025, there is no excuse for a cleaning company to be unable to prove that work was done. If a provider cannot answer the question "how do you prove you cleaned my facility?" with anything beyond "trust us," keep looking.

Leading-edge providers now offer:

  • QR code audit trails — Staff scan location-specific QR codes at the start and end of each task. Timestamps and GPS are recorded.
  • Digital checklists — Task-by-task sign-off with photographic evidence for corrective actions.
  • Client dashboards — Real-time visibility into completed tasks, compliance scores, and issue resolution — accessible 24/7.
  • Automated reporting — Monthly PDF reports generated automatically, removing the need to chase your cleaning company for updates.

EagleWing's Command™ dashboard provides all of the above. Ask any prospective cleaner to demo their reporting system — it takes five minutes and tells you everything about how seriously they take accountability.

4. Check Compliance Credentials for Your Facility Type

Different facility types require different compliance frameworks. Using a general office cleaner in a medical facility, food production environment, or school is a liability risk.

  • Medical and healthcare — Look for ACHS accreditation familiarity, TGA-listed disinfectants, documented infection control protocols, and colour-coded microfibre systems (AS/NZS 4146).
  • Schools and childcare — Staff should hold current Working With Children Checks (WWCC) and relevant National Police Checks. Products used must be child-safe.
  • Industrial and warehousing — Providers should have current Safe Work Method Statements (SWMS) and WHS compliance documentation specific to your site's hazards.
  • Food production — HACCP-familiar processes, food-grade cleaning agents, and scheduled audits against Food Standards Code requirements.

Ask for the specific documentation relevant to your facility type before signing. A compliant provider will have it ready.

5. Assess Responsiveness Before You Sign, Not After

The responsiveness of a cleaning company's sales team is your most accurate preview of their operations team. Run these tests during the quote process:

  • How long did they take to respond to your initial enquiry?
  • Did they conduct an in-person site inspection before quoting, or quote over the phone?
  • How quickly did they follow up after the site visit?
  • When you asked a detailed question (insurance, staffing, technology), did they answer directly or deflect?

A company that takes three days to send a quote and struggles to answer basic compliance questions will not be faster or more responsive once they have your contract.

Frequently Asked Questions

Commercial cleaning costs in Melbourne vary based on facility size, frequency, and scope. Office cleaning typically ranges from $35–$65 per hour. Most quality providers charge per service or per square metre rather than hourly. Always request a site-specific quote rather than a phone estimate.

At minimum: public liability insurance ($20M+), professional indemnity insurance, and workers compensation. Ask for certificates of currency — not just verbal confirmation — and check the expiry dates.

A cleaning company employs staff directly and carries full employer obligations (super, insurance, training). A contractor may subcontract work, which can affect accountability, training standards and insurance coverage. Ask specifically whether the staff who will clean your site are direct employees.

Technology-forward providers use QR code audit systems, timestamped digital sign-offs, and client dashboards that provide real-time visibility into completed tasks. Ask any prospective cleaner how they prove service delivery — vague answers are a red flag.
